Phrase bank

Two pairs · compare the meanings

Pair 1

jump the gun

Act too soon; do something before the right time (origin: athletics).

jump on the bandwagon

Join others in doing something popular, often without thinking it through.

Contrast: one is about timing (too early); the other is about following the crowd.

Pair 2

throw in the towel

Give up; stop trying because you accept defeat (origin: boxing).

throw your hat in the ring

Announce that you want to take part in a competition or challenge (origin: boxing).

Contrast: one means quit; the other means enter the fight.

Speaking

Discuss the differences

As in the coursebook — compare the two idioms in each pair. Use the phrase bank if you need support.

jump the gun / jump on the bandwagon

  • When might someone jump the gun at work or with a project?
  • When have you (or people you know) jumped on the bandwagon?
  • Can you follow a trend without jumping on the bandwagon?

throw in the towel / throw your hat in the ring

  • What is the difference between giving up and stepping forward to compete?
  • Describe a situation where someone threw in the towel too soon.
  • When would throwing your hat in the ring be a brave thing to do?
