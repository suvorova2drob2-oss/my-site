Participle clauses

A

Types of participle clause

1.

Present participle clauses

Hoping to learn more about eating healthily, I signed up to a vegetarian cooking course.

NB: Stative verbs can be used in present participle clauses.

2.

Perfect participle clauses

Having researched all the options, she settled on the yoga retreat in Corsica as it had the best reviews.

3.

Passive participle clauses

Having been given a stark warning by his doctor, Jim realised he had to make serious lifestyle changes.

4.

Verbless clauses

Alone and afraid, she wrapped herself in a blanket.

B

Use of participle clause

1.

To refer to simultaneous events

Running down the street for the bus, I tripped over.

replaces a while clause
2.

To refer to earlier events

Having spoken to the fitness instructor, I have a clearer idea of what his classes will involve.

replaces an after clause
3.

To refer to a reason

Not wanting to wake anyone else up, I snuck out of the room and quietly closed the door.

replaces a because clause
4.

To refer to a condition

Given a choice, I’d rather dance than go to the gym.

replaces an if clause
5.

To provide information about a noun phrase

The message posted today was about meditation techniques.

replaces a defining or non-defining relative clause

NB: Unlike relative clauses, these can often come before the noun phrase: Built in 1066, the castle is a major tourist attraction.

C

Adding a subject to a participle clause

1.

If actions happen at the same time or give extra information

Her eyes filling with tears, Yasmin was clearly overwhelmed at her gold medal win.

2.

When with is used to introduce a different subject

With exercise classes running throughout the day, the centre is perfect for everyone’s schedule.

3.

To add a subject without with

There being no further matters to discuss, we closed the meeting early.

D

Worked examples — combining sentences

Misplaced modifier (joke)

After finishing my meal, the waiter handed me the dessert menu.

I looked at him and said, ‘Can I ask you something?’

‘Certainly,’ he replied.

‘Why did you eat my meal?’

Fixing the joke

With me finishing my meal, the waiter handed me the menu.

Having finished my meal, I was handed the menu by the waiter.

Earlier event — perfect participle

They had enough money.

They decided to book premium economy tickets.

Having enough money, they decided to book premium economy tickets.

Reason / background — with or having

There were so many films to choose from. They knew they’d spend ages flicking through all the channels.

With so many films to choose from, they knew they’d spend ages flicking through the channels.

Having so many films to choose from, they knew they’d spend ages flicking through the channels.

Verbless / optional being

He was tired after a long day. He headed straight to bed.

(Being) tired after a long day, he headed straight to bed.

Same subject vs different subject; reduced relative

Everybody looked at the man. He was causing a massive scene in the restaurant.

With the man causing a massive scene in the restaurant, everybody looked at him.

Causing a massive scene in the restaurant, everybody looked at the man.

Everybody looked at the man who was causing causing a massive scene in the restaurant.

Perfect vs present participle

She finished the book. She decided to write a review about it.

Having finished the book, she decided to write a review about it.

Finishing the book, she decided to write a review about it.

Passive participle / post-modifying noun

The apartments were a nightmare to sell. They were built on a toxic waste site.

Built on a toxic waste site, the apartments were a nightmare to sell.

The apartments, built on a toxic waste site, were a nightmare to sell.

NB: With the apartments built — not used here (draft note from the book).
